## About me

I am an atmospheric scientist at the **National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM)**. My research focuses on the physical mechanisms governing **tropical atmospheric circulation, convection, precipitation, and climate variability**, with particular interest in Mexico, Mesoamerica, the Caribbean, and tropical South America.

I investigate how large-scale climate modes and tropical disturbances interact across timescales, from intraseasonal variability to interannual climate fluctuations. I combine atmospheric dynamics, observational and reanalysis datasets, numerical-model output, and scientific computing to understand regional hydroclimate, predictability, and weather-related risks.

## Research interests

- **Tropical dynamics and convection** — Convectively coupled equatorial waves, tropical circulation, and organized convection.
- **Climate variability and teleconnections** — El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO), the Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO), and the Quasi-Biennial Oscillation (QBO).
- **Subseasonal-to-seasonal prediction** — Rainfall variability, rainy-season characteristics, and the Mesoamerican midsummer drought.
- **Tropical hydroclimatology** — Atmospheric moisture transport, low-level jets, and precipitation in complex regional environments.
- **High-impact weather and climate risk** — Thunderstorms, lightning climatology, and the interaction between natural hazards and social vulnerability.

## Current scientific work

My current computational research includes:

- **ENSO evolution:** Historical behavior of El Niño and La Niña events, including persistence, duration, transitions, and changes in event characteristics.
- **Lightning in Mexico:** Spatial and seasonal lightning climatologies, severe-convection environments, and lightning-related risk.
- **Tropical variability:** The role of tropical waves and large-scale circulation in organizing rainfall and influencing regional predictability.
